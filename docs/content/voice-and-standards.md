# SDK Enterprises — Voice & Content Standards

How SDK Enterprises writes. Applies to the public website, the client portal,
email and any customer-facing text. Reads like experienced engineers speaking
directly to serious business clients.

## 1. Voice

- **Human.** Written by people for people. No corporate insulation, no
  committee phrasing.
- **Direct.** Say what we do and what it costs, in that order.
- **Concise.** One idea per sentence. Cut every word that does no work.
- **Specific.** Name the technology, the problem, the constraint, the
  deliverable. "We migrated a 600-service estate to Kubernetes" beats "we do
  cloud modernization".
- **Technically credible.** Correct terms, correct spelling of frameworks and
  standards. When in doubt, use the fewer, verifiable claim.
- **Commercially understandable.** The buyer (often not an engineer) must
  grasp the outcome, the engagement model and what happens next.

## 2. Banned phrases

Never use — and do not substitute equivalent buzzwords:

cutting-edge solutions · innovative solutions · unlock your potential ·
tailored solutions · seamless solutions · drive innovation · future-proof ·
our team of experts · world-class · end-to-end solutions · game-changing ·
best-in-class · agile (as a filler) · synergy · leverage (as a verb, unless
about a tool) · robust (alone, unqualified) · scalable (alone, unqualified)

**The paste test:** if a sentence could be pasted onto another software
consultancy's site unchanged, it is too generic. Rewrite it with a specific
problem, mechanism, or outcome.

## 3. Headings communicate an idea

Headings are not labels; they say what the section means to the reader.

- Bad: "Our Services" / "Contact Us" / "About Us"
- Good: "Use all of the stack — or only the part you need." /
  "Tell us what you're trying to build, modernize, automate or fix." /
  "Senior engineering without unnecessary layers."

A good heading states the benefit, the promise, or the honest framing of what
follows. Use sentence case (capitals only for the first word and proper nouns)
unless the reference explicitly uses all-caps display styling.

## 4. Every section must answer a real question

Before writing a section, state its purpose. Useful questions a section can
answer:

- What does SDK do?
- What problem does this solve?
- Why does it matter to this reader?
- When would someone hire SDK?
- Why should they trust SDK?
- What happens next?

If a section answers none of these, cut it or reframe it.

## 5. Evidence & claims

- **Never invent** customers, testimonials, statistics, outcomes, partnerships
  or claims. A claim without a verifiable source is removed, not reworded.
- Capability phrasing is allowed ("we build X", "we operate Y"); quantified
  results are not, unless they are real and attributable.
- The approved Canva references contain fabricated proof points ("600+
  applications…", banking/telecom case studies). They are **visual reference
  only** and must not appear in copy. Real, verifiable company facts (name,
  SIREN/SIRET, address, email, phone, France-based) may be used.
- Technologies are **evidence, not the product.** Explain the problem and the
  capability first; list the stack as proof of depth, never as the headline.

## 6. Copy review gate

First-pass copy is never automatically final. Every piece of copy is reviewed
by a separate pass that checks:

1. Paste test — is any sentence generic enough to live on any consultancy site?
2. Banned-phrase scan (grep the list in §2).
3. Claim audit — every statistic, customer, partnership or outcome is
   verified or removed.
4. Heading check — every heading communicates an idea (§3).
5. Section purpose — every section answers one of the questions in §4.
6. Consistency — tone, terms and capitalization match the rest of the site.
